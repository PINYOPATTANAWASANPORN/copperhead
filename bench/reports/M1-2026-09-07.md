# M1 — up to six copper layers

The first multilayer measurement (`add-multilayer-layout`, ADR 0011): the three multilayer golden microboards through routing (track B) with every wrapped router, the copper stack read from layer names, through vias only, and the four- and six-layer fabrication profiles. Synthetic boards; the claim is that the harness handles the stack, not a real-board number.

- Date: 2026-09-07
- Harness: copperhead 0.10.0 on the `add-multilayer-layout` working tree, kicad-cli 10.0.4, node v24.18.0, linux x64
- Suite: `bench/suites/multilayer-microboards.json`, routers `router-freerouting`, `router-kicad-tools`, `router-orthoroute`, ensemble, 300 s per board, one engine at a time
- Run: `bench/var/runs/M1-multilayer`

## Numbers

| board | layers | outcome | selected | completion | wire | vias | DRC errors | engine time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| four-layer | 4 | PASS | Freerouting | 100 % | 143 mm | 10 | 0 | 4 s |
| six-layer | 6 | PARTIAL | Freerouting | 95 % (1 owed) | 771 mm | 20 | 0 | 74 s |

Selection regret 0, invalid-over-valid 0, harness overhead 13.4 s per board.

## What the engines did

- **Freerouting** routes the four-layer board clean and uses the inner layers (the gated live test asserts copper on In1.Cu and In2.Cu and every via a through via). On six layers it leaves one of nineteen connections owed on the congestion netlist, with no DRC error: four inner layers did not remove the last bottleneck at the QFN.
- **kicad-tools** reaches 33 % on four layers and declines the six-layer board on its auto-grid rule (the `declined` kind of ADR 0010).
- **OrthoRoute** returns blind vias (F.Cu to In2.Cu) on both four-layer boards; the harness rejects each invocation as `INVALID_OUTPUT` naming the via and net, so no candidate is built from it. It routes with blind and buried vias by default and is unusable here until those are supported. On six layers it returned no copper.
- **via-span** is a verify expectation, not a routing one: track B strips the seeded copper before routing, so here it is the four-layer board again. `check` on the committed file reports `geom.via-layers` on both buried vias, and KiCad DRC reports two dangling vias; the refusal suite carries the case.

## What M1 establishes

- The stack, the via span in connectivity, the profiles by count, the layer count handed to engines, and the renders all work end to end on four and six layers; every two-layer golden board regenerates byte for byte and every two-layer expectation and B1 to B4 number stands.
- What it does not: a real-board number. The four-layer PCBench suite (task 7.4) waits on disk for the corpus clone. The six-layer board is synthetic.

## Reproduce

```bash
npx tsx bench/golden/generate.ts
copperbench run bench/suites/multilayer-microboards.json --max-parallel 1 --out bench/var/runs/M1-multilayer
```
