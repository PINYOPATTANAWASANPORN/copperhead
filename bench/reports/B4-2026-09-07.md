# B4 — closed loop

Milestone B4 of RFC 11 §13.5 (`openspec/changes/add-pcb-layout-framework`, task 7.7): the loop runs unattended on supported boards and fails explicitly elsewhere. Directional until 30 held-out boards exist; this report says what the loop did on the boards it has.

- Date: 2026-09-07
- Harness: copperhead 0.10.0 at `becc205`, kicad-cli 10.0.4, node v24.18.0, linux x64
- Loop: `copperhead pcb layout` (`layoutBoard`): staged placement (pyplacer and the fixed control, rule stages, intent), staged routing (Freerouting, kicad-tools), verification, then up to three repair cycles under the deterministic policy (no model in the bench), 300 s per microboard, 600 s per real board, one engine at a time
- Suites: `layout-microboards` (track C, the 15 golden boards end to end) and `repair-pcbench` (track D, the eight PCBench boards that did not route clean in one pass at B1, placement kept, repair cycles only)

## What the numbers say

**Track C.** Thirteen of the fifteen golden microboards end PASS in one cycle: the placers and the four rule stages clear every seeded placement fault (the overlap, the part off the board, the connector away from its edge, the part inside the keepout ring, the blocks too close together), and the staged routing plan routes the rest clean. The edge stage moved a part on five of the fifteen boards, by 0.2 to 5 mm, always after pyplacer or the fixed control had left it inside the copper-to-edge clearance. Selection regret is 0 and no invalid candidate ever outranked a valid one.

The two PARTIAL boards are the two routing microboards built to stay owed:

- `congestion` fans sixteen signals through one channel. The staged plan routes the power nets, spends its generous share, and the pass at the rule reaches 95 % in cycle 0, the same figure Freerouting alone reached at B1. The planner then finds no action whose estimate fits what is left of the 300 s, and the run ends PARTIAL with two connections owed.
- `decoupling-qfn` owes two connections under a QFN. The policy walks the catalog (keep the copper and route the owed nets, more passes, each router in turn, the owed nets first); every cycle returns 67 %, kicad-tools' candidate fails the routing gate and is rolled back, the best board is kept, and the run ends PARTIAL with the history recorded. Nothing in the catalog moves the capacitor, which is what this board needs; a model-driven `move-group` is the action the bench does not take.

No board ended HOLD: no hard intent constraint was violated by every candidate.

**Track D.** The eight PCBench boards that did not route clean in one pass at B1, with the authors' placement kept and 600 s each. One ends PASS (the crossover, which the staged plan routes clean where both engines reached 67 % in B1's single pass). Seven end PARTIAL, and the loop repaired none of them:

- `AnalogThermometer` (92 %, two owed) and `sensorboard_programmer` (56 %, five owed) get the full catalog for owed connections: `continue-routing`, more passes, each router in turn, the owed nets first. Every Freerouting cycle returns the same completion; kicad-tools' candidate fails the routing gate on the first and produces no copper on the second, and both are rolled back to the best board. The connections these boards owe are ones no wrapped router completes at the authors' placement, and the catalog's placement actions (`move-group`, `rotate-component`) are not offered for owed connections without a model.
- `rufs_smart_psu` never has a candidate that passes the routing gate: every cycle's best board carries shorts. Two `rip-up-nets` cycles on the nets the shorts name reduce the error count from 32 to 20, and no third rip-up fits the budget. The board is left unchanged, as the contract requires.
- `flypi v2` (97 %, two owed), `hp_led_switch`, `beehive` and `piezo_amplifier` spend the whole 600 s in cycle 0: on these boards Freerouting runs its stage allotment to the end, the stage at the rule gets the other half, and nothing is left for a repair. At the first B4 attempt `flypi v2` routed clean because the staged run overran its budget (every stage had the whole limit); bounded to 600 s it reaches 97 %. The three others end with every candidate failing a hard gate (13 to 103 errors: shorts and clearance on dense boards the wrapped routers do not handle at this version) and the board unchanged.

Selection regret is 0 and no invalid candidate outranked a valid one on either track. No run ended HOLD, and no run claimed a result a gate refused: the seven PARTIAL boards are reported as such with the owed connections and the errors named.

## What B4 establishes, and what it does not

- The loop runs unattended end to end on both corpora, keeps the best board across cycles, rolls back a worse cycle, records every action with its outcome, and stops within its budget. That is the B4 criterion, and it holds.
- Repair cycles have not yet turned a PARTIAL into a PASS on any board. The deterministic policy's routing actions do not change what Freerouting can complete at a given placement, and the placement actions that would (`move-group`, `rotate-component`) are only reachable through a model turn, which the bench does not run. The repair loop's value on real boards is therefore unmeasured until either a model-in-the-loop bench (`--model`) or a deterministic placement action for owed connections exists.
- The eleven loop defects listed below were all found by these runs, none by the unit tests that preceded them. Every one is now covered by a test, and both tracks were run again after each fix: the numbers above are from the eighth run of each.

## What the first pass of B4 found and fixed

The first end-to-end runs (before the numbers above) exposed three defects in the loop itself, each fixed and covered by a test before the runs were repeated:

- A repair cycle that ended worse than the best result so far (an engine that refused the board, a timeout) became the final answer. The loop now keeps the best board and outcome across cycles; the failed action stays in the history.
- The staged routing plan preserved the board's existing copper on every stage, so a seeded short survived a rip-up. The first stage now honours the rip-up, and the composite candidate carries only what the stages routed.
- Freerouting echoes every preserved wire back in its session file, and the staged composition appended them again: a 35-part board came back with 1266 segments where 447 were real, and every wirelength figure from a staged run was inflated. The composite now keeps one copy of each piece of copper. Single-mode runs (B1, B2) were not affected.

The later passes, with those fixed, found eight more, each fixed and covered before the next run; in order:

- The two boards with a seeded placement fault (a courtyard overlap, a part off the board) were refused after pyplacer had cleared the fault: it parked a part inside the copper-to-edge clearance, and no rule stage pushed it back. A fourth rule stage now moves a part the least distance in from the edge; both boards end PASS.
- kicad-tools refuses a dense board at 0.2 mm clearance on its own safety rule (its auto-grid cannot go finer than clearance/2 within its memory budget). The harness recorded that as an engine crash, and the planner spent two more cycles on the same router. The refusal is now its own failure kind, `declined`; a board every engine declines is UNSUPPORTED rather than ENGINE_ERROR, and a router that made a cycle worse is dropped before the next action.
- The per-cycle record counted owed connections on the outcome, which drops info-severity findings, so every cycle read "0 owed" on boards owing two. The count now comes from the selected candidate's full findings, and a rolled-back cycle also restores the run the planner reads, so the next action is planned over the best board rather than the failed one.
- Cycle 0 handed routing the whole remaining budget, and on the congestion board one Freerouting pass at the rule ran for 4.5 of the 5 minutes: the board owed two connections and no repair cycle could run. A cycle that might be followed by a repair was made to leave half the budget for it. Two further runs then measured that rule: two real boards that route clean in one full-budget pass (flypi v2, AnalogThermometer) end at 97 % and 92 % on a half share, `continue-routing` does not recover them because Freerouting needs the whole time on the nets it finds hard, and no repair cycle on either corpus has yet turned a PARTIAL into a PASS. The first pass takes the whole budget again; the repair cycles run on what remains.
- Inside one staged run the same thing happened one level down: Freerouting spends its whole allotment on a board it cannot finish, so the generous bulk stage took the run's budget and the stage at the rule never started (congestion at 26 % where a single pass at the rule reaches 95 %). A stage before the last now gets at most half of what the run still holds; the final stage takes the rest.
- A staged routing run gave each of its five invocations the whole run limit and never checked its own budget between stages: on three PCBench boards two kicad-tools passes of five minutes each spent the 600 s budget inside cycle 0. Each invocation is now clamped to what the run still holds, and an engine the budget cannot afford is recorded as not started rather than run.
- With that clamp in place, a staged run whose last stage could not start threw away the copper its earlier stages had routed: the congestion board ended TIMEOUT with no candidate and nothing for the planner to act on. The candidates now come from the last stage that produced copper, so a cut-off run yields a partial board the repair cycles can work from.
- With half the budget saved for repair, no repair fit: the catalog priced every routing action at what the last pass cost, which was the whole share, so four PCBench boards and the congestion microboard ended cycle 0 with 300 s unspent and "no action fits". The catalog now has `continue-routing`, which keeps the copper so far and routes only the owed nets at half the last pass's cost; the policy offers it first for owed connections.

## What "unattended" means at B4

- No model ran: the planner's deterministic policy chose every repair action. With `--model`, the same catalog is offered to one JSON-validated model turn per cycle, and the policy stands whenever the answer is not a valid catalog action.
- Explicit failure: a seeded placement fault that no rule stage or placer resolves ends the run in `REFUSE` before any engine runs (pre-flight and placement gates) or in `HOLD` when the planner's only remaining move is to ask (a hard constraint no catalog action can meet). Nothing is marked complete while a gate fails, and the user's board is never written unless `--apply` is given and the outcome is `PASS` or `PARTIAL`.
- Evidence: every run directory carries every cycle's placement and routing runs with their candidates, provenance, and diagnostics; the outcome records the cycles.
