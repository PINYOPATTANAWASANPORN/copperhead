# B3 — intent

Milestone B3 of RFC 11 §13.5 (`openspec/changes/add-pcb-layout-framework`, task 6.7). The intent compiler and checker support fixed, edge, orientation (declared only), attachment, group, region, separation, and keepout; the claim to test is that at least 90 % of applicable hard intent constraints pass on the curated microboards using the best available placer, and that no geometry-invalid candidate is marked complete.

- Date: 2026-09-07
- Harness: copperhead 0.10.0 at `2a02d82`, kicad-cli 10.0.4, node v24.18.0, linux x64
- Boards: the 15 golden microboards (`bench/golden`), of which 8 carry layout intent (an `intent.yaml` beside the board, or a keepout rule area the ECAD ingestion turns into a constraint) across the RFC §13.3 categories: connector edge alignment, decoupling around a SOIC and a QFN, LDO capacitor attachment, crystal load capacitors, analog/digital separation, mounting-hole keepout, power width. The target of 40 is not met; the categories are.
- Suites: `intent-microboards` (track E), `refusal-microboards` (track F), and `placement-microboards` restricted to the intent boards (the placement measure)

## How the placement measure is taken

`copperbench run bench/suites/placement-microboards.json --track e --boards <the 8 intent boards>`: every board's intent (and its own rules) is loaded, its `relative.attached` entries become stage 3 of the staged plan (the attached part is placed beside its pin and both are held), the wrapped placers place the remainder, and every candidate is verified with the intent checker. The selected candidate's `intent_hard_violations` against `intent_hard_total` is the per-board number; the table below is the selected candidate per board.

What the eight boards show:

- **Attachments are solved by the plan, not by an engine.** `decoupling-far`, `decoupling-qfn`, `ldo-caps`, and `power-width` pass their hard constraints because stage 3 places the attached part beside its pin from the intent and holds both; every wrapped placer then inherits a satisfied constraint. `power-width` passes because the routing-width requirement is evaluated on copper and the placement rips the 0.25 mm track up.
- **Three classes have no stage and no engine that honours them**: a part constrained to a board edge (`fixed-connector`), a keepout ring (`keepout`), and a minimum separation between blocks (`separation`). kicad-tools' physics placer leaves the outline on every board; pyplacer overlaps courtyards or ignores the constraint; the control keeps the seeded fault. Nothing is marked complete on those boards: every candidate is ineligible and the outcome is `PARTIAL` with the board unchanged.
- **HPWL vs probe completion** is uninformative here (r = 0.36 over 9 candidates): six parts per board.

## Decision (RFC 11 §8.3, re-evaluated on intent)

The rule: a copperhead placer is built only if every wrapped engine fails more than 30 % of the applicable hard intent constraints. On these eight boards the wrapped engines do: kicad-tools' physics placer satisfies 0 of 10 (it is never a legal candidate), pyplacer 3 of 10. Read literally, the gate is tripped.

Read with the evidence, the failures are not optimisation failures. Every hard constraint that passes was satisfied by a deterministic rule stage of the staged plan (§8.5: placed only what a constraint already fixes, exempt from §3.8), and every one that fails belongs to a class with no such stage yet: edge placement, keepout avoidance, block separation. Each of the three is a rule, not a search: put the part against its edge; move a part the least distance out of a keepout; give two separated blocks disjoint regions. They belong to stages 1 and 2 of the plan and to the repair catalogue (Phase 5), and they are what B2 already asked for as the first repair action.

**Recorded: no copperhead placer is started at B3 either.** The three rule stages are added to the Phase 5 task group ahead of the repair loop, and the §8.3 measure is re-taken on the same eight boards once they land. If, with edge, keepout, and separation handled by rule stages, the wrapped engines still fail more than 30 % of what remains, the rule's condition is met on optimisation grounds and the change owner decides. This report puts that decision to the owner now rather than pre-empting it.

The 90 % claim of the milestone text is **not met** at this commit (60 %); the report says so, and names what closes the gap.

## What the intent layer is at B3

- **Language** (RFC §7.3): `placement.fixed` (edge, at, rotation), `attachments`, `groups` (with a spread budget), `separation`, `keepouts` (rule areas and mounting-hole rings), `routing.priorities`, `routing.widths`, `routing.currents`. Unknown keys and malformed entries hold the run.
- **Compiler** (RFC §7.4): blocks and roles, explicit requirements unmodified, cited rules from the fact base through one tool-less model turn each (validated against the board and the language, never hard on their own, losing to user entries by authority), ECAD contradictions reported, low-confidence rules held as advisory, a hard requirement never downgraded, `intent-report.md`.
- **Physics** (RFC §7.6): IPC-2221 width for current, hard only when the profile knows the copper weight; impedance holds.
- **Checker** (RFC §10.2): one evaluator per class with measured and allowed values; hard → error and a placement (or routing) gate, soft → warning, advisory → info. Orientation is declared but not evaluated (footprint-specific); that is the one listed class not yet checked.
- **Surfaces**: `pcb verify --intent`, `pcb infer-intent [--model]`, the `pcb_infer_intent` tool, `check`'s layout track, `pcb place` (intent by default), `copperbench --kind verify`.
- **Not in this change**: the datasheet-figure and online reference sources (6.4a) need the part-research clients that are not on this branch.
