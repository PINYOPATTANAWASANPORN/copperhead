# B2 — placement baseline

Milestone B2 of RFC 11 §13.5 (`openspec/changes/add-pcb-layout-framework`, task 5.5). Wrapped placers behind the one contract, thirty placement cases runnable, a fixed-router probe downstream of every candidate, the evidence on whether HPWL predicts routing completion, and the RFC §8.3 decision recorded.

- Date: 2026-09-06
- Harness: copperhead 0.10.0 at `d59700e`, kicad-cli 10.0.4, node v24.18.0, linux x64
- Placers (every candidate re-verified by the harness; the probe routes each gate-passing candidate once with Freerouting 2.4.1 at 10 passes and default rules):
  - `placer-kicad-tools-physics` 0.20.0 (`kct placement optimize --strategy force-directed`, MIT, deterministic)
  - `placer-pyplacer` at `34baa02` (BSD-3-Clause, vendored with two patches, seeded)
  - `placer-fixed`: the control, returns the input placement
  - not in the suites: `placer-kicad-tools-evolutionary` (exits 1 upstream at this version, ADR 0008) and `placer-reference` (harness only)
- Cases: `bench/suites/placement-microboards.json` (the 10 golden microboards, every part movable) and `bench/suites/placement-pcbench.json` (the 20 PCBench qualification boards, re-placed from their authors' placements, the human baseline), 30 in all
- Mode `ensemble`, one engine at a time (`--max-parallel 1`), 300 s per board, scoring `default-placement-2-layer` (courtyard overlaps and off-board parts are gates; routability first, then HPWL, then congestion)

- **The control is beaten on three boards.** pyplacer shortens HPWL by 25 to 60 % on `congestion`, `clearance`, and `open` with equal probe completion; on `congestion` it also removes the five probe DRC errors the committed placement produces. That is the placement uplift the milestone asks to be reported, not claimed in advance: three of ten.
- **The gates do the work.** pyplacer's five rejected candidates are real: parts pushed against the board edge (`copper_edge_clearance` on three boards), a courtyard overlap with a through-hole inside it, and a part outside the outline on the keepout board. kicad-tools' physics placer leaves the outline on every board (ADR 0008) and never becomes a candidate.
- **Shorter is not always better.** On `decoupling-far` pyplacer halves HPWL (32.8 against 72.0 mm) and the probe then routes only half the nets; the control's longer placement routes fully and wins. HPWL alone would have picked the wrong board.
- **HPWL does not predict routing completion on boards this small** (r = 0.03 over 12 eligible candidates): everything routes, or fails for a reason HPWL cannot see. The PCBench half of this table is the meaningful sample; see below.
- Three golden boards end `PARTIAL` because every candidate fails a gate: `overlap` and `outside-board` are seeded placement faults the placers were asked to fix and did not (pyplacer moved the parts clear but onto the edge), and `keepout` needs the intent checker (Phase 4) to be placed around.

- **The human placement wins all twenty.** `placer-fixed` (the authors' placements) is the only engine to pass the placement gate on any real board. pyplacer produced a candidate on 17 boards and every one has a courtyard overlap (its annealer packs by bounding box, not courtyard), five also sit on the copper-to-edge clearance, two leave the outline; on three of the larger boards it ran past the 300 s budget. kicad-tools' physics placer leaves the outline on all 15 boards it finished and timed out on the other five, as on the microboards.
- **pyplacer's wirelength is often shorter and never legal.** On 12 of its 17 candidates its HPWL beats the author's (down to a third on `rufs_simple`, `temp_breakout`, `hp_led_switch`), which is exactly why a gate-free ranking would be dangerous: every one of those boards would have been handed back with overlapping parts. Selection regret and invalid-over-valid stay at zero because the gates run before the score.
- **HPWL predicts completion across real boards** (r = −0.65 over the 20 eligible candidates, one per board): the probe routes the small, short-HPWL boards fully and the large ones partly (`beehive`, `piezo_amplifier`, `rufs_smart_psu` at 0 to 14 %). With one candidate per board this is a board-size effect as much as a placement effect; the within-board comparison the RFC wants needs a second legal placer, which this run did not have.
- **Cost.** Harness plus probe time per board averages 158 s, almost all of it the Freerouting probe and KiCad DRC on the larger boards; the placers themselves average under 15 s. The probe budget is the lever if this becomes a routine step.

## Decision (RFC 11 §8.3)

The rule: a copperhead placer is built only if every wrapped engine fails more than 30 % of the applicable hard intent constraints. Intent constraints (fixed, edge, orientation, attachment, group, region, separation, keepout) arrive with the Phase 4 checker, so the rule cannot be evaluated on intent yet. On the geometry gates the evidence is unambiguous and worse than the threshold: on the 20 real boards neither wrapped engine produced one legal candidate (pyplacer 0 of 17, kicad-tools 0 of 15); on the 10 microboards pyplacer produced a legal candidate on 5 and beat the committed grid on 3. Every pyplacer rejection is one of two things, a courtyard overlap or a part on the edge clearance, and never a wiring problem.

Recorded decision: **no copperhead placer is started at B2.** The §8.3 gate is re-evaluated at B3 on intent compliance, with this geometry record attached. Two things change before then, neither of which is a placer:

1. The repair loop (Phase 5) gets its first concrete catalogue entry from this run: a deterministic legalization step that moves an overlapping or edge-hugging part the minimum distance clear, applied to a wrapped engine's candidate before re-verification. Per §3.8 it is evaluated against the neighbours first (`kct placement` has an overlap resolver; KiCad's own push is not scriptable) and adopted or wrapped if one fits.
2. The routability probe's budget becomes a config knob; at 158 s per real board it is the dominant cost of a placement run.

kicad-tools' physics placer stays registered and fails closed; it is not usable at 0.20.0.

## Reproduce

```bash
bench/corpora/tools.sh          # JRE 25, freerouting-2.4.1.jar, kicad-tools venv
bench/corpora/pcbench.sh        # PCBench at dec3be7, qualification boards upgraded
copperbench run bench/suites/placement-microboards.json --track d --max-parallel 1 --out bench/var/runs/B2-placement-microboards
copperbench run bench/suites/placement-pcbench.json --track d --max-parallel 1 --out bench/var/runs/B2-placement-pcbench
```

Both `report.html` files carry the RFC 11 §13.4 record; `summary.csv` has the per-candidate metrics the table above is drawn from.
